# About where the forge is going

This page was put together from the positions of the forge's intent
(`projects/forge/10-intent.md`) that speak of its direction: how far
the chain is to grow, how new kinds of artefact are added, which
reviewers are planned, who the forge is for, and at what pace it is
developed. It is for a user, an extender or an evaluator who wants to
know where the forge is heading and how firm that is.

## Direction, not destination

The forge is a general tool for forging thoughts, and a run ends where
its owner is satisfied: the outcome is an artefact the owner stands
behind, nothing further. The forge itself is developed at its owner's
discretion and pace, by his needs and by the feedback it gets.
Direction may be named; destinations and deadlines are not.

## The chain grows downward

The owner intends to keep extending the chain downward, as far as he
needs a thought taken:

- a BRD layer is certain to come;
- solution architecture and integration are intended;
- a strategy layer is possible, if it proves to make sense.

Which layers are added, and in what order, is open: possibly all of
them, possibly none yet. Nothing is approved for construction. The
mechanics of a layer, such as its commands, its agents and the
calibration of its reviewers, are designed when that layer is actually
taken up, not in advance. A layer below the assignment grows in the
same project, by the same principal's hand, when he chooses to take
his own thought further. A chain never spans two principals.

## New kinds of artefact

A new kind of artefact is added by one command, `/new-artefact`, so
that the chain can grow without anyone having to know by heart what a
kind of artefact is made of. The command leads to everything a kind
needs and forgets none of it: what the artefact is, for whom, from
what it is derived and why; its definition and its template as a
pair; the prefixes of its items where it needs its own; whether it
needs a challenger of its own and what the critic must know of it;
and where it is realised, in the solution design. The command decides
nothing: each of these is born on the owner's word. The first
artefact of the new kind is made on real work, and that is the trial
of its definition.

The reason for one command: a kind added by hand took a search for
everything it touches, and two of the things it needs, a prefix and a
challenger, are easily forgotten.

For now the command adds a kind to the engine, for every user of it.
A user is to be able to add an artefact of his own just as easily,
kept at his own place. Where that place is remains open.

## Independent challengers

Challengers that run on a different model family than the author's
are planned, so that a challenge can bring genuinely different priors.
The direction is decided; the mechanics are designed when the work is
taken up.

## One principal per instance

For now the forge is a single-user tool per instance: more people
mean more instances, with coordination by git.

## Feedback and ideas

The forge is open to those who use it. What it wants of them most is
feedback and ideas. A finished change is welcome too, on the forge's
own rule: a change of how the forge behaves goes through the chain
before it is built, a document that is a render goes through its
recipe, and nobody sends a change he has not tried himself. What
enters the forge stays the owner's decision.
