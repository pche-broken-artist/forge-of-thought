# What the forge is and is not

Forge of Thought is a place for forging thoughts: a workshop where a
thought is tempered and shaped into documents that someone can act
on. This page says what the forge is, what it is deliberately not,
and why it is built that way. It is for anyone who meets the forge
for the first time, whether to use it, to extend it or to judge it.

## What it is

The forge is a general engine. Any idea can enter it: a process
redesign, a platform initiative, an organisational topic, anything
else. The idea travels a chain of versioned documents, from the idea
put together onward, under review by isolated reviewers who never
see the working conversation. The course of that journey is told on
[How a thought travels through the forge](how-a-thought-travels.md).

The forge is bound to no person and to no management relationship.
The **principal** is whoever's thinking is being forged. The
**recipients** are whoever receives the result: a team, a colleague
or the principal's own future self. Who the principal of one
installation is counts as an instance fact (a fact of one
installation), not a property of the system. The forge began as one
person's tool; that origin is an instance fact too, and not the
engine's identity.

Claude is the principal's cognitive extension. "Cognitive extension"
means an amplifier of the principal's thinking, never a substitute
for it. Claude owns structure, order, process discipline and document
hygiene. The principal owns the content and every decision about it.
Claude's contribution to content is to criticise, challenge, inspire
and lay out options. The principal composes: nothing enters the
content because Claude proposed it, only because the principal took
it up. The two roles and the rule between them are the matter of
[The principal and Claude](the-principal-and-claude.md).

## What it is not

The forge is not a program. Nothing is implemented in it. The engine
specifies: its product is a chain of documents, and at the far end of
the chain stands a specification ready for realisation, not the
realisation itself.

The forge is not a delivery tool. A run of the forge ends where its
owner is satisfied. The outcome is an artefact the principal stands
behind, and nothing further. Sponsorship, funding, adoption and
delivery outcomes are outside the forge's scope and outside its
sight. An intent may even be immaterial and carry no delivery at all.

The forge is not a chat. State lives in files, never only in the
conversation. What is in a file survives the session, that is, one
sitting of the conversation with Claude. What lives only in the
conversation does not survive it.

The forge is not tied to one domain. The mechanics of the chain are
domain-agnostic. The same machinery that forges a platform assignment
would forge a role-playing campaign: an intent of the story, then
materials rendered for the game master.

## How it works

The chain starts at a brief and its trunk is the intent, the document
of what the principal wants. Below the intent a project takes the
layers it needs, and many projects end at the intent. The chain grows
by adding the definition of a layer, without reworking anything that
exists. The full chain the forge aims at runs from a raw idea all the
way to a deck ready for realisation, layer by layer: an assignment,
then a business requirements document, then a solution architecture,
up to an implementation-ready specification including integration.

One design constraint shapes almost everything else. An assignment
must carry the complete in-scope substance of the intent, written as
well and as precisely as it can be. Nothing is left out for brevity's
sake. Length is whatever fidelity requires. What keeps an assignment
an assignment rather than a solution is the kind of its content,
never the amount.

## Why

**Why the name.** Thoughts are the raw material of the forge, and
nothing is presumed about them: they may be of any kind. Forged
artefacts are the product, an assignment among them. The name was
chosen with the full chain in mind, from assignment through
architecture to a realisation deck. Other names were considered and
rejected before it: one because it literally named the waterfall,
one because it collides with existing products, and several more
that fell against the name chosen. In daily speech the short form
"Forge" is expected; in documents the full name stands.

**Why "thought" and not "assignment".** An assignment is one place a
chain may end, not the goal. Any subtitle or one-line description of
the forge must therefore present it as a place where thoughts are
forged and must not name the assignment as the goal. The founding
framing, a tool of one person for briefing the people who report to
him, was dropped for the same reason: it described the first
instance, not the engine.

**Why the chain ends where the project needs it to.** A run ends
where its owner is satisfied, so a project takes only the layers that
satisfy its owner. Many thoughts are done once the intent stands.
Because the forge is meant to grow layer by layer, its files and IDs
are numbered with gaps, so that a layer can be added without renaming
anything.

**Why not a delivery tool.** Delivery is outside the forge's sight,
and the forge does not pretend to see it. The door stays open rather
than closed: the forge may one day feed a delivery chain, and
experience from use flows back to the principal as input like any
other. Whether a delivery side would be grown layers of the forge or
a separate framework is deliberately undecided. The direction, and
what is not promised, is the matter of
[Where the forge is going](where-it-is-going.md).

**Why state lives in files.** A file survives the session and the
conversation does not. Whatever is to last must therefore be written
down, and nothing is reported as safe while it lives only in the
conversation.

**Why no implementation.** The engine specifies and does not build.
The reason beyond that statement is not recorded.

## A worked example

A principal has a half-formed thought: the weekly planning meeting of
her group takes two hours and settles nothing, and she suspects the
meeting is the wrong shape rather than the people the wrong ones. She
brings that thought to the forge. The brief captures it as it is,
half-formed. The intent is mined out of it over several rounds:
positions on what the meeting is for, facts about who attends and
what is decided there, a rejected direction (abolishing the meeting
altogether, dropped because the decisions it makes have nowhere else
to go). Isolated reviewers press on the intent from different angles,
and the principal answers each point or parks it.

After a few rounds the intent stands and the principal is satisfied.
Her recipients are herself and two colleagues who run the meeting
with her, and an intent they all stand behind is enough for them to
change the meeting on Monday. The run ends there, at the intent, with
no assignment and no later layer. Nothing is missing: the chain ended
where this project needed it to. Had the thought been a platform
initiative handed to a supplier, the same project could have grown an
assignment below the intent, and further layers below that, without
reworking what already stood.
