# What the forge is and is not

Forge of Thought is a place for forging thoughts: a workshop where a
thought is tempered and shaped into documents someone can stand
behind. This page says what the forge is, what it is not, and why it
is so, for anyone who meets it for the first time: the person who
wants to use it, the one who wants to extend it, and the one who
wants to judge it.

## What it is

The forge is a general engine for forging any thought. An idea of
any kind travels a [chain](how-a-thought-travels.md) of versioned
documents, from the idea as it was first put together onward, under
isolated adversarial review. The subject matter is deliberately
unconstrained. The owners name as examples:

- a process redesign
- a platform initiative
- an organisational topic
- anything else

The forge is bound to no person and to no management relationship.
It began as one person's tool. That origin is an instance fact, that
is, a fact of one installation, not a property of the system. Who
the principal of an installation is, and what language the
conversation runs in, are instance facts as well. They live in a
local file of the installation that never enters the engine.

Two roles work in the forge, and the rule between them is on
[The principal and Claude](the-principal-and-claude.md).

The **principal** is whoever's thinking is being forged. The
principal supplies ideas, answers and decisions, and is the final
authority on all content. The recipients of what the forge produces
may be teams, colleagues, or the principal's future self.

**Claude** is the principal's cognitive extension, not a supplier.
Cognitive extension means an amplifier of the principal's thinking,
never its substitute. Claude owns structure, order, process
discipline and document hygiene. Its contribution to content is to
criticise, challenge, inspire and lay out options. The composition is
the principal's. Nothing enters the content because Claude proposed
it, only because the principal took it up. In one sentence: Claude
proposes and never decides.

Beside Claude stand isolated reviewers: agents that are deliberately
blind to the working conversation and press on the documents from
different angles.

The chain ends where the project needs it to. Below the
[intent](../reference/glossary.md), the trunk of every project, a
project takes the layers it needs, and many projects end at the
intent. The chain grows by adding the definition of a layer, without
reworking anything that exists. A forge run ends where its owner is
satisfied: the outcome is an artefact the principal stands behind,
and nothing further.

## What it is not

**Not a program.** The forge runs in Claude Code, the command-line
agent for Claude. Its slash commands, skills, agents and hooks are
the mechanisms of that agent, and the forge is what they are told.
Nothing is implemented in the forge: the engine specifies.

**Not a delivery tool.** A run ends where its owner is satisfied. An
intent may be immaterial and carry no delivery at all. Sponsorship,
funding, adoption and delivery outcomes are outside the forge's
scope and outside its sight. The door stays open rather than closed:
the forge may one day feed a delivery chain, and what is learned in
use flows back to the principal as input like any other. Whether a
delivery side would be grown layers of the forge or a separate
framework is deliberately undecided.

**Not a chat.** State lives in files, never only in conversation.
What is in a file survives the session, that is, one working
conversation in Claude Code. What lives only in the conversation
does not survive it, and the forge says what that is before a
session ends. One project per session is the hygienic default.

**Not tied to one domain.** The mechanics of the chain are
domain-agnostic. The owners put it in one image: the same machinery
that forges a platform assignment would forge a role-playing
campaign, an intent of the story first, then materials rendered for
the game master.

## Why

**Why a general engine.** The founding framing, a tool of one person
for briefing the people reporting to him, was dropped as the
system's identity. It describes the first instance, not the engine.
The general-engine framing superseded it, and anyone can be the
principal.

**Why an amplifier and not a substitute.** The principal composes.
He assembles what the forge offers into his own positions and
decisions. So that the content stays his, every decision about
content remains the principal's, while Claude keeps structure,
order and process discipline.

**Why the name.** The system is named Forge of Thought because
thoughts are the raw material, of whatever kind, and nothing is
presumed about them, while forged artefacts are the product, an
assignment among them. Other names were considered and set aside:

- "Cascade": it literally names the waterfall
- "Foundry": it collides with existing products of that name
- "Continuum", "Strata", "Idea Forge" and a name in another
  language meaning a smithy: considered, not chosen

The full name stands in documents. The short form "Forge" is
expected in daily speech.

**Why "thought" and not "assignment".** The name was chosen with the
full chain in mind: from a raw idea all the way to a deck ready for
realisation, growing layer by layer. An assignment is one place a
chain may end, not the goal. So that the name does not shrink the
chain to one of its layers, any subtitle or one-line description of
the forge must present it as a place where thoughts are forged, and
must not name the assignment as the goal. The subtitle the forge
carries is "A workshop where thought is tempered and shaped."

**Why the chain ends where the project needs it to.** The ambition
is the full chain, and every design decision is made with that
growth in mind. Files and item IDs are numbered with gaps, so that a
layer can be added without renumbering. But a layer is taken only
when a project needs it: the outcome of a run is an artefact the
principal stands behind, and many projects end at the intent. Where
the forge is developed from here is on
[Where the forge is going](where-it-is-going.md).

## A worked example

A principal has a thought: the way his team hands work between
shifts loses information, and he wants to change it. He puts the
thought together as a [brief](../reference/glossary.md), a few
paragraphs in his own words. From the brief, by questions and
answers with Claude, an intent is found: the positions he holds, the
facts he states, the directions he rejects, and the matters still
open. The intent goes through several versions, isolated reviewers
press on it, and he takes up what he finds worth taking up.

At the fourth version he is satisfied: the intent says what he
wants and why, and he stands behind it. He runs the hand-over
himself, so there is no assignment to write and no team to brief.
The recipient of this run is his future self. The run ends at the
intent, and the project has no layer below it. Whether the new
hand-over keeps the information it was meant to keep is outside the
forge's sight: that is delivery, and what he learns from it comes
back to the forge only as a new input of his.
