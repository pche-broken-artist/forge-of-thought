# About what the forge is and is not

This page says what Forge of Thought is, what it is deliberately
not, and why it carries the name it does. It is for anyone who meets
the forge for the first time, whether to use it, to extend it or to
judge it. It was put together from the engine's core instructions
(`CLAUDE.md`) and from the forge's own intent
(`projects/forge/10-intent.md`): the identity and the two roles from
the first, the reasons from the second.

## What it is

Forge of Thought is a place for forging thoughts: a workshop where
thought is tempered and shaped. An idea of any kind, a process
redesign, a platform initiative, an organisational topic, anything,
travels a chain of versioned documents from the moment it is put
together onward, under adversarial review by reviewers that are kept
isolated from the working conversation.

It is a general engine, not bound to one person or one management
relationship. It began as one person's tool, and that origin is now
an instance fact, not the system. Two words name the parties:

- The **principal** is whoever's thinking is being forged. The
  principal supplies ideas, answers and decisions and is the final
  authority on all content.
- The **recipients** are whoever receives the result. They may be
  teams, colleagues, or the principal's future self.

Who the principal of a given installation is lives in a local file
of that installation, never in the engine itself.

Claude is the principal's cognitive extension, not a supplier. Claude
owns structure, order, process discipline and document hygiene; the
principal owns content and every decision. Claude's contribution to
content is to criticise, challenge, inspire and lay out options; the
composition is the principal's, who assembles what the forge offers
into positions and decisions of his own. Nothing enters the content
because Claude proposed it, only because the principal took it up.
"Cognitive extension" therefore means an amplifier of the principal's
thinking, never its substitute: Claude proposes and never decides.

## What it is not

**Not a program.** Nothing is implemented in the forge; the engine
specifies. What comes out of a chain is a document the principal
stands behind, not working software.

**Not a delivery tool.** A forge run ends where its owner is
satisfied: the outcome is an artefact the principal stands behind,
nothing further. An intent may be immaterial and carry no delivery
at all. Sponsorship, funding, adoption and delivery outcomes are
outside the forge's scope and outside its sight. The door is left
open rather than closed: the forge may one day feed a delivery
chain, and experience from use flows back to the principal as input
like any other; whether a delivery side would be grown layers of the
forge or a separate framework is deliberately undecided.

**Not a chat.** State lives in files, never only in a conversation.
A session can be ended at any point without loss, because nothing
depends on what a conversation remembers; the next session
re-orients itself from the files.

**Not tied to one domain.** The chain's mechanics are
domain-agnostic. The same machinery that forges a platform
assignment would forge a role-playing campaign: an intent of the
story, then materials rendered for the game master. The subject
matter is deliberately unconstrained and nothing is presumed about
the thoughts that enter.

## Why the name

The system is named Forge of Thought because thoughts are the raw
material, of whatever kind, with nothing presumed about them, and
forged artefacts are the product, an assignment among them. The
subtitle is "A workshop where thought is tempered and shaped": any
one-line description must present the forge as a place where thoughts
are forged and must not name the assignment as the goal, because an
assignment is one place a chain may end, not the goal.

Other names were weighed and dropped. "Cascade" literally names the
waterfall. "Foundry" collides with existing products of that name.
"Continuum", "Strata", "Idea Forge" and "Kovárna" were other
candidates considered before Forge of Thought was chosen.

### Why "thought" and not "assignment"

The name says thought, not assignment, because the assignment is
only one of the things a chain may produce. The ambition behind the
engine is the full chain: from a raw idea as far as a deck ready for
realisation, growing layer by layer. An assignment is one layer of
that chain, and a name that stopped at it would misname the whole.

### Why the chain ends where the project needs it to

Below the intent a project takes the layers it needs, and many end
at the intent. A layer a project does not have is not missing. The
chain grows by adding a layer's definition, without reworking
anything that exists, which is why every design decision is made
with growth in mind and why files and IDs are numbered with gaps.
Where a chain ends is the project's to say, in the same way that
where a run ends is its owner's: at the artefact the principal
stands behind.
