# About what the forge is and is not

This page explains what Forge of Thought is, what it deliberately is
not, and why it carries the name it does. It is for anyone who uses
the forge, extends it or is weighing whether it fits: it was put
together from the forge's operating rules (`CLAUDE.md`, the sections
on what the workspace is and on roles) and from the forge's own
intent (`projects/forge/10-intent.md`, its Essence, its positions on
the collaboration model, on state, on naming and on the growth path,
and its rejected directions), stated as the intent reasons them.

## What the forge is

Forge of Thought is a place for forging thoughts: a workshop where
thought is tempered and shaped. Any idea, whether a process redesign,
a platform initiative, an organisational topic or anything else,
travels a chain of versioned documents from the idea put together
onward, under isolated adversarial review.

It is a general engine, not bound to one person or to one management
relationship. It began as one person's tool, and that origin is an
instance fact, not the system. The words it uses are general on
purpose:

- **The principal** is whoever's thinking is being forged. He supplies
  the ideas, the answers and the decisions, and has the final word on
  all content.
- **The recipients** are whoever receives the result: teams,
  colleagues, or the principal's own future self.

Who the principal of a given installation is, and what language the
conversation runs in, are facts of that installation. They are kept
apart from the system and never written into it.

## Claude's place in it

Claude is the principal's cognitive extension, not a supplier. Claude
owns structure, order, process discipline and the hygiene of the
documents; the principal owns the content and every decision. Claude's
contribution to the content is to criticise, challenge, inspire and
lay out options; the principal assembles what the forge offers into
his own positions and decisions. Nothing enters the content because
Claude proposed it, only because the principal took it up. "Cognitive
extension" means an amplifier of the principal's thinking, never its
substitute. Claude proposes and never decides.

## What the forge is not

**Not a program.** Nothing is implemented in the forge. The engine
specifies: what comes out of it are documents that say what is wanted,
not the built thing.

**Not a delivery tool.** A forge run ends where its owner is
satisfied: the outcome is an artefact the principal stands behind,
nothing further. An intent may be immaterial and carry no delivery at
all. Sponsorship, funding, adoption and delivery outcomes are outside
the forge's scope and outside its sight. The door is left open rather
than closed: the forge may one day feed a delivery chain, and what is
learnt in use comes back the ordinary way, through the principal, as
input like any other. Whether a delivery side would grow as further
layers of the forge or as a separate framework is deliberately left
undecided.

**Not a chat.** State lives in files, never only in the conversation.
A session can be ended at any point without loss, and work picks up
again from the files, so nothing depends on what a conversation
remembers.

**Not tied to one domain.** The subject matter is deliberately
unconstrained, and the chain's mechanics are domain-agnostic: the same
machinery that forges a platform assignment would forge a role-playing
campaign, with an intent of the story and then materials rendered for
the game master.

## Why the name

The system is named Forge of Thought because thoughts are its raw
material, of whatever kind, with nothing presumed about them, and the
forged documents are its product. The short form "Forge" is expected in
daily speech. Other names were weighed and dropped: "Cascade" because
it literally names the waterfall, "Foundry" because it collides with
existing products, and "Continuum", "Strata" and "Idea Forge" among
others were considered before Forge of Thought was chosen.

An earlier framing described the forge as one person's tool for
briefing the people who report to him. It was dropped as the system's
identity because it describes the first installation, not the engine;
the general-engine framing replaced it.

## Why "thought" and not "assignment"

The name was chosen with the full chain in mind: from a raw idea all
the way to material ready for realisation, growing layer by layer
through an assignment, a business requirements document, a solution
architecture and an implementation-ready specification. The assignment
is only where an early version of the chain happened to end. That is
why any subtitle or one-line description must present the forge as a
place where thoughts are forged and must not name the assignment as the
goal. The subtitle is "*A workshop where thought is tempered and
shaped.*"

## Why the chain ends where the project needs it to

Below the intent, a project takes the layers it needs, and many end at
the intent. The chain grows by adding a layer's definition, without
reworking anything that exists. Every design decision is made with
that growth in mind, which is why files and identifiers are numbered
with gaps, and why the name says thought, not assignment.
