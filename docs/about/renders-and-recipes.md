# Renders and recipes

A render is an audience-specific output generated from the documents
of a project, and a recipe is the file it is generated from. This
page explains what the two are, how an output is made from them in
two steps, and why a render is never edited by hand. It is for anyone
who uses the forge, extends it or judges it.

## What it is

A render is an output made for one audience from the chain of a
project, that is, from its versioned artefacts such as the intent and
the assignment. Examples are a pitch, an architecture picture, an
executive summary and the README of the repository. A render is
never a source of truth: the artefacts of the chain stay the only
source of truth. A render assigns nothing and is not part of the
chain.

A recipe is the file a render is made from, `recipes/<recipe>.md`.
It holds in one versioned file:

- the inputs: one artefact or several, by path;
- the audience;
- the instructions: tone, emphasis, what to omit;
- the format, where the recipe wants a file beyond the Markdown;
- the literal template of the output, with placeholders.

More than one input is legitimate, such as the intent and the
assignment together. A render may cite another render as a picture
source. This holds only when the citing recipe declares that render
among its inputs. A recipe is composed in conversation with the
principal (the person whose thinking is forged) and iterated; how is
said in [Compose a recipe](../use/compose-a-recipe.md). A render is
never edited by hand: what is iterated is its recipe, and the render
is regenerated from it, as [Render an
output](../use/render-an-output.md) shows.

The boundary between the chain and a render is authorship, not
audience. A chain artefact is composed by the principal, with Claude
proposing. A render is generated from artefacts. An article the
principal writes is a layer of the chain; its translation is a
render.

Three kinds of file are not renders, though they are generated or
lie beside them:

- a page of the documentation, made from a map and not from a
  recipe, as [The documentation](the-documentation.md) says;
- a work product, an output the project makes with work of its own,
  as [The work product](the-work-product.md) says;
- the two fixed files of the root, the security policy and the code
  of conduct, as [The security policy and the code of
  conduct](the-security-policy-and-the-code-of-conduct.md) says.

## How it works

### Regeneration and provenance

`/render <recipe>` regenerates the output from the recipe
mechanically. The output is undated and overwritten freely. Every
render opens with its provenance: the recipe and every input, each
with its version. A render is stale when a version its provenance
cites differs from the version now on disk; the provenance block and
the definition of stale are in [Render
provenance](../reference/render-provenance.md).

A render is regenerated only by `/release` or by the principal's
explicit `/render`. Claude never regenerates a render on its own
judgement: it reports the staleness and offers. The README and the
release notes are regenerated by every release. Every other render is
as stale as the principal lets it be. No check reports the staleness
of a render; the state map of the project (`/forge`) shows it. A
release that regenerated renders reports in its summary what
materially changed in them.

### The recipe as a versioned tool

A recipe is a tool, not a record of thinking. It carries a version in
its front-matter and no status. It stays at 0.x unless the principal
names an integer such as 1.0 or 2.0. When he names one, the recipe
takes it and the count goes on from there, 2.1, 2.2. Claude never
proposes the integer. The number means nothing beyond the principal's
own mark: nothing guards it and nothing reads it. The date of a
recipe is computed from its history, and a render cites the version.

Being versioned, a recipe keeps its history in a companion beside it,
`recipes/<recipe>.history.md`, like every versioned document. The
companion records what changed in the recipe; the substantive turns
of the thinking live in the intent.

A recipe is deliberately loose. Its template fixes the structure and
what information appears where, never the wording. A presentation
recipe says what belongs on a slide, not its phrasing. Each rendering
re-derives the words from the current inputs. One exception is
allowed: a recipe may pin load-bearing wording as fixed text, such as
a title, a claim or a fixed line. What is pinned regenerates
verbatim; everything else re-derives freely.

### Several languages

A recipe may declare its output languages in `languages:`. The first
is primary, the rest are its translations. `/render` generates the
primary render and, in the same run, translates it into every other
language, scene for scene and line for line. The result is one render
per language, `renders/<recipe>.<lang>.md`. Each has its own
provenance and its own row in the Renders table of the ledger (the
file that says the state of a project, made at read time). A
translation cites the primary render it was made from. Where the
recipe names a format, the format file follows the name of its
language. A recipe with one language keeps `renders/<recipe>.md`.

### The two steps of an output

Everything the forge produces is Markdown, renders included: a
presentation is a Markdown file saying what is on each slide, with
mermaid for pictures. The forge ends at content, but it carries its
delivery-format tools at its edge. An output is made in two steps,
each with its own command.

The first step is `/render`. It generates the Markdown and, where the
recipe names a format, the plain file beside it in `renders/`:

- a deck render becomes a PowerPoint file, through pandoc;
- a document render becomes a Word file, through pandoc;
- a screenplay render becomes a video, or the events its player
  plays, through the screenplay script; the screenplay, its two
  formats and where a public one lands are in [The screenplay and
  its player](the-screenplay-and-its-player.md).

This step is deterministic, cheap and repeated freely.

The second step is `/publish`. It makes the designed file through a
model and its document skills, into `published/`. It runs only on the
principal's command: never by `/render`, never by `/release`, never
on Claude's own judgement. It makes a file and sends nothing
anywhere. It takes the render as it lies on disk and never renders.
A stale render is named before the conversion, and the word is the
principal's. The provenance of the designed file stands in a
companion beside it, `published/<recipe>.md`; the Published table of
the ledger reads it and says what each published file was made from
and whether it is current or stale. How the step runs is in [Publish
a designed file](../use/publish-a-designed-file.md). A screenplay has
no designed step.

The format and what each step needs stand in the recipe's `## Format`
section. They are never copied into the render. A recipe that names
no format ends at the Markdown. A plain file and a published file are
tracked in git like any render output and never edited by hand: the
Markdown render stays the source of truth. Every other format
conversion stays outside the forge.

### The principal's own files beside the forge's

The forge owns in `renders/` and `published/` only the files its
recipes name: the render and its translations, the plain file, the
designed file and its companion. Any other file there is the
principal's own, made by hand from the forge's files. Examples are:

- a copy under a new name that he edits;
- a PDF saved from Word or PowerPoint;
- an HTML of a diagram.

Such a file is kept in git beside the forge's files. It has no
companion, no row in the ledger and no check. The forge never reads
it, never overwrites it and never raises it. The forge's own file
stays untouched by hand: what is to change is copied first.

### The README and the release notes

Every project has a README as a render of its own
`recipes/readme.md`, with the output in the project root. A thought
project also has release notes, from `recipes/release-notes.md`. The
engine's own README and release notes are made by the same
mechanism. Every `/release` of the project regenerates both after its
check. The README is never edited by hand: a fix of its content goes
into the recipe or the inputs, and the file is regenerated. Both
recipes are genres of `/recipe` and are scaffolded by `/new-project`.

A README presents the project to a human meeting its repository for
the first time: a recipient, a colleague, the principal after weeks
away. It stands alone, so that no claim requires opening the chain.
It carries what the thing is, what one gets, how to start, where it
stands and where the documentation is. Every claim is derivable from
the inputs; nothing is invented, and nothing superseded in the inputs
survives. It closes with a short section saying that it is a render,
regenerated at every release and never edited by hand, followed by
one fixed sentence that points to the engine the project runs under.
The engine's own README is the one exception to that sentence,
because it is the destination the sentence points to.

The release notes are a log of releases, not a story. They have one
section per release, newest first, with fixed groups in a fixed
order and one sentence per change from the user's side, with a
pointer. The sections are derived at the release from the records of
the history since the previous release. One thing is never derived:
what the reader must do is written with the change in the `Action`
field of its record, and the render carries it word for word. At a
major version the minors since the previous major are folded into
it. A library has a README only, a catalogue of what it holds and
how to use it, from its ledger and indexes.

### Genres

A recipe may be composed through a genre interview, `/recipe
<genre>`. Each genre has one definition carrying the elicitation
checklist and a skeleton, `templates/recipe-<genre>.md`, that
extends the base shape of a recipe and never replaces it. The
genres today are `presentation`, `readme`, `release-notes`,
`screenplay` and `user-stories-export`.

## Why

A render is never edited by hand because the text of a render is
re-derived from the current inputs at every rendering. Fixing the
text in the template would turn the recipe into the render and make
the inputs meaningless. The boundary is drawn at authorship, not
audience, so that what the principal composes stays in the chain and
what is generated stays outside it.

A render is regenerated only on the principal's word or by a release
because regeneration is stochastic. The same inputs never guarantee
the same words, so an unreviewed regeneration is an unreviewed edit
of an outward-facing document. Two light defences follow from this,
neither of them a gate: the pinned wording of a recipe, and the
report of a release on what materially changed. A rendering of only
the stale renders at every save was considered and rejected. It
would save little on the engine and nothing on a project, whose
README input, the ledger, moves at every operation. It would also
add a staleness state that the save, the state map and the checks
would all have to read alike.

A recipe carries no status because approval has a meaning only where
someone receives the document, and a recipe is received by nobody.
It is iterated continuously. An integer the principal names is
therefore his own mark and not an approval.

The translations of a recipe are made in the same run as the
primary render because the quotes of a transcript, the beats and the
captions are chosen once. A second generation would choose them
again, differently, and the translations would no longer share one
cut.

An output is made in two steps divided by cost so that the expensive
conversion runs as seldom as possible. `/publish` never renders
because a render is made by a model and is never the same twice: a
second one would publish a text the principal has not read.

The reason the principal's own files are admitted beside the forge's
in `renders/` and `published/` is not recorded. The rule that the
forge's own file is copied first keeps the forge's file untouched by
hand, as every render is.

The release notes are a log because a reader wants to see plainly
what was added, changed and removed. A library has no release notes
because release notes are derived from the history of an intent,
which a library does not have; its history is git.

The pages of the documentation are not renders because a page is to
come from the project's documents with no hand in between, while a
recipe is a tool the principal shapes. Many pages would mean as many
recipes to iterate by hand; the documentation map replaces them,
with one generated entry per page.

## A worked example

A recipe of a generic project, an executive summary of the intent in
two languages, made into a Word file, has this shape:

```markdown
---
project: my-project
purpose: an executive summary of the intent
audience: the recipients of the assignment
version: 0.3
languages: en, de
---

# Recipe — an executive summary of the intent

## Inputs
- 10-intent.md
- 20-assignment.md

## Instructions
- One page for a reader who has not opened the chain.
- Carry the objective and the positions that bound the work;
  omit the open threads.
- Plain, direct; no claim beyond the inputs.

## Format
- Format: docx
- Plain file, made by `/render` through pandoc: reference none,
  page size A4.
- Published file, made by `/publish` through a model: template
  none, model <model>.

## Template
# <Project title> <intent version>

## Objective
<one paragraph from the intent's objective>

## What is wanted
<one line per position in scope>
```

From this recipe `/render` writes `renders/summary.en.md` and, in the
same run, `renders/summary.de.md`, each opening with its provenance,
and beside them `renders/summary.en.docx` and
`renders/summary.de.docx` through pandoc. On the principal's word,
`/publish` makes `published/summary.en.docx` through a model and
writes its provenance into `published/summary.en.md`. If the
principal then saves a PDF from Word, he keeps it in `published/`
under his own name; the forge leaves it alone.
