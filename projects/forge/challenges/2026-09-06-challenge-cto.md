## Overall read
As written at 3.45, this intent is two things wearing one name. The first — the chain from brief to assignment under blind review — has not gained a position since 2.8 (18 August); the assignment-style positions POS.0200–0290 date from 15 August. The second — a governed operating layer for Claude Code: git doors, releases, release notes, history companions, contract skills, checks, a skills migration — is where all forty-five versions since 3.0 (eight days) went, each built and verified the same day on the forge itself by its author, while the two subject projects' intents stand at 0.9 (since 4 August) and 1.5. The thinking inside the second thing is careful and mostly right. My concern is that the intent still describes itself as the first; that its primary ambition — the chain growing downward, a BRD "certain to come" — rests on an assumption the stated first audience, a company rollout, breaks; and that the mechanism has just shown me, from inside, an instance-fact leak the intent has parked as a small risk.

## Challenges

### CHL.0140 — The chain grows downward across principals and repositories, and every mechanism assumes it does not
- **Severity:** major
- **Challenge:** The Essence and POS.0700 make the full chain the primary ambition — assignment, then BRD, then solution architecture, numbered `30-`, `40-` in one project. Every mechanism assumes the layers are written by the same principal in the same repository: `/forge <state>` works one project; a new layer "branches from any artefact by adding one file"; the assignment is "the only document handed over"; feedback from recipients has no channel (POS.0170); the essence lens reads each layer against its parent in the same directory. In the first audience the brief names — rollout in the company — the BRD is written by the recipient, in his own instance (POS.1110: one user per instance, more people means more instances), from the assignment he received. The chain's next layer is then born as another principal's brief in another repository, and nothing in the intent says how an assignment becomes a brief, how the ID scheme continues (his REQ.0010 against the assignment's REQ.0010), where the essence lens finds its parent, or what happens to his BRD when the assignment goes to 1.1. THR.0090 says multi-principal use is "deliberately not being worked on"; POS.0700 says the BRD layer is certain. They are the same question, held as certain on one page and refused on another. DEC.0060 deferred a layer's *mechanics* to the day it is taken up; this is not mechanics but whose hand and which repository the layer is in, which decides whether the `/forge` star, the ID scheme and the parent-child reading of the chain survive the first BRD at all.
- **Why it matters:** The full-chain ambition justifies the name, the numbering gaps and "every design decision below". If the layers below the assignment live in other people's instances, the growth path is not a set of state files in this repository but a handover protocol between instances, and its cheapest choices are the ones made now (what an assignment carries so that it can serve as a brief; whether a project can be seeded from an artefact). If instead the principal means to write the BRD himself in the same project, the rollout audience receives an engine whose defining ambition is the author's private use.
- **What would change my mind:** A stated position on which of the two shapes the growth path takes — same principal, same repository; or a new project of a new principal seeded from the assignment — and, for the second, one worked case on a subject project: an assignment handed to a colleague's instance as his brief.
- **Epistemic status:** my judgement

### CHL.0150 — THR.0230 is being decided by accretion, in the direction that makes the later decision most expensive
- **Severity:** major
- **Challenge:** Since 3.0 the intent added POS.1000–1140 and rewrote POS.0310, 0550, 0570, 0730, 0810, 0840, 0930 and 1070 — every one an engine matter by THR.0230's own list (git through scripts, the ledger, versioning and history, isolated agents, recipes and renders, indexes, save, check, setup). No position about the chain — what an intent is, what an assignment carries, what a reviewer of substance looks for — has changed since 2.8. THR.0230 defers the boundary "after an outline of the second framework" and records that the principal is not sure a common engine is a good idea. But the boundary is being drawn every day, by writing engine positions into the forge intent in the forge's vocabulary, interleaved with chain positions; the first draft of a second framework will meet a 1,779-line document from which the engine must be extracted by rewrite. The three signals from other users the intent records — a newcomer's setup struggle (POS.1050), a colleague's reviewer contract (POS.1120), a critic that hunts formalities (POS.0410) — are all about the tool; none is about an assignment. The principal's hesitancy is the tell: whether a common engine is a good idea is already answered by what his rounds are spent on.
- **Why it matters:** Two failure modes. If the engine is never named, the forge carries the wrong name and the wrong README for what is being built — a governed operating layer for Claude Code with a thin chain on top — and the rollout attracts users for the layer, not the chain, as the recorded feedback already shows. If it is named later, the cost is a rewrite proportional to what accreted in between — at the current pace roughly thirty-five lines of intent a day.
- **What would change my mind:** Either the decision that there is no engine — the forge is the whole and THR.0230 closes — or a decision on where the line runs, taken before the next twenty positions rather than after. Evidence of a chain position worked in the last three weeks would weaken the direction claim.
- **Epistemic status:** my judgement; version counts read from the history companion

### CHL.0160 — The upgrade channel has one user, who never upgrades
- **Severity:** major
- **Challenge:** `forge-pull` is a fast-forward of `main`; a project records no engine version; `/check` measures against current conventions (POS.0940; brief condition 3). The author never experiences an upgrade: he writes the convention and migrates the forge project in the same session. What another instance meets on pulling 3.0 → 3.45, eight days apart: the Version History moved out of every versioned document (a `/check` finding on each of his projects, migration "on the principal's word" — his projects, his word, no script); a Notes block required at the tail of every history row, without which his project's release notes cannot be compiled; every project's release-notes recipe reshaped; `/check-forge` gone and `/check` re-cut into four checks, one of them now running at every `/save`; every command moved from `.claude/commands/` to skills, so anything he added locally sits in a layout the engine abandoned (THR.0300 has no home for it yet); `disable-model-invocation` on the writing commands. The "Action required" group of the release notes exists for him, written by the one person who never has to act on it. The forge's own research found that frameworks that reach into user projects end in "manifests, override layers and migration commands"; the forge avoided file migrations and got convention migrations instead — the same cost, paid by hand and without a tool.
- **Why it matters:** The rollout audience is a set of instances that will pull rarely and each time meet several weeks of this. The rational response is the one already observed: a colleague grows his own system from the forge rather than tracking it (POS.1120). Then `forge-pull` is not an upgrade channel but a fork point, and every Action required line is one more reason not to pull.
- **What would change my mind:** One instance other than the author's that pulled across at least two releases, with a record of what it had to do and how long it took. Or a stated rule on churn between majors — a user migrates only at a major, and minors carry no Action required.
- **Epistemic status:** my judgement; the field evidence is the forge's own research of 2026-08-29

### CHL.0170 — Every isolated reviewer receives the instance facts and writes an immutable public file; THR.0210's parking premise is false today
- **Severity:** major

I confirm it from inside. My context carries the principal's name and role, his two commit identities with their hosts — one of them a company host — and his private memory notes. Nothing in my instructions tells me not to write any of it into this file, which is immutable from creation, lives in `projects/forge` and goes public at the next `/release`. THR.0210 was parked on the ground that "the risk is small while he knows of it", to be taken up "when the boundary is next at stake — a reviewer run on `projects/forge`". This run is that case and every reviewer run on the forge project will be. The fix of FND.0250 removed the instance fact from the persona file and proposed passing the principal's role in the task prompt; the task does not carry it, so the persona knows whose peer it is only through the leak it is not supposed to have. Isolation here rests on an accident of the harness: `CLAUDE.local.md` was chosen as the home of instance facts because Claude Code reads it, and Claude Code reads it into every subagent too.
- **Why it matters:** The public boundary (POS.0980) was bought once by rewriting immutable documents and starting a fresh history; a second breach costs a second history rewrite, and the 2026-09-04 force-push shows the class is real. The reviewers are the one writer in the system that is both blind to the conversation and unread before its file is immutable — that is where the leak will happen, not in a command the principal watches.

Or the reviewers' isolation designed rather than inherited: what an isolated agent sees of the instance decided, what it may write about it stated, and the one instance fact a persona needs — the principal's standing — passed on purpose.
- **Epistemic status:** consensus on the mechanism (observed in my own context); my judgement on the weight

### CHL.0180 — The intent has become the chronicle it was designed not to be, and the always-on layer grows with it
- **Severity:** major
- **Challenge:** POS.0120 makes the intent "the consolidated current state … the document to read when returning to a project after weeks"; prime directive 7 puts structure over prose. At 3.45 the intent body is 1,779 lines, about 40,000 tokens. The positions written this week are decision narratives: POS.1120 runs sixty-three lines and carries the dates of a trial, the line counts of three agent files before and after, which findings were settled with it and what a colleague's system does; POS.0730, POS.0310, POS.1100 and POS.0180 run forty to fifty lines each in the same manner. That is the content the kinds table (POS.1080) assigns to history, decisions and research. POS.0310 moved 8,477 words of history out of the body on 4 September; the body has been refilling with history since. CLAUDE.md, the always-on copy, went 435 → 523 → 636 lines between 29 August and today while THR.0240 says it is "to be dealt with sooner or later", and the last three engine checks returned ten, seven and nine findings, most of them the two copies drifting. The thinking error is treating size as hygiene — a critic's matter — when it is a design property: the forge states every rule with its full rationale and the story of how it was reached, in the layer every command, every render and every reviewer loads.
- **Why it matters:** The measured cost is paid on every reviewer run and every render (THR.0240's own numbers). The unmeasured cost is that a document nobody reads whole is no longer the current state of anything: it is trusted, not read, and the next contradiction lives in it for a release until a critic finds it. What a position is — a stance, or the record of reaching it — is a decision the intent has not taken and is taking by default.
- **What would change my mind:** A stated rule on what a position carries and what goes to the records, with the intent measured against it once. Or a measurement that reviewer and render quality is unaffected by the size — the trial of THR.0270 measured the context, not its effect.
- **Epistemic status:** my judgement; the numbers read from the files

### CHL.0190 — Release 4.0 is next in the queue, and nothing says what an approved major of this intent means
- **Severity:** minor
- **Challenge:** POS.0300 inherits "integers denote signed-off versions" from a BRD standard in which sign-off has a counterparty. This project has none: no assignment, no recipient; the handover artefact is the operating layer itself. 3.0 was approved "after the split was executed"; the ledger queues 4.0 after this challenge is settled. What 3.45 must satisfy that 3.44 did not is stated nowhere — not that the operating layer is built to every position, not a fresh-clone verification, not a second instance having run it. The major has real consequences all the same: the tag `v4`, forty-five minor sections folded into one, a release-notes head reading "approved". A sign-off without a test is a date with an integer.
- **Why it matters:** The user of the engine reads the major as the safe point to pull to — the tag exists "so that `/check` and the release notes can rely on it". If a major means only that the principal felt done, the signal is noise for the one reader it is for.
- **What would change my mind:** A stated test a major of the forge intent must pass, whatever it is, applied to 4.0 before the tag.
- **Epistemic status:** my judgement
