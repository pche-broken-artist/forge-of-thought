# What of the intent belongs to an assignment

## Question

The forge project has no assignment: its ledger header says
`terminal: intent`, its Documents table marks `20-assignment.md` as
not planned, and THR.0470 answered on 2026-09-29 that no assignment
is made for the forge because it would be a third copy beside the
intent and the operating layer, and precise wording has its home in
the intent until the file that performs it exists.

The principal asks the question again as a thought experiment:
imagine the forge project had an assignment. Is there anything in the
intent that would rather belong to it? Would the intent save any text
if an assignment existed beside it?

The note answers with counts. It reads the intent as it stands at
4.41, classifies every item by the kind of content it carries, and
separates three numbers: what leaves the intent under the cleaning of
THR.0470 step 4 regardless, what would leave only because an
assignment exists, and what an assignment would duplicate. It defends
neither the status quo nor an assignment.

## Method and epistemic status

**Counted (verifiable).** The intent at 4.41 has 2 363 lines; the
line numbers of every `##`, `###` and item heading were taken by
search, so the number of items per group and the lines per group are
exact: 110 positions, 22 rejected directions, no facts, 27 lines of
Essence, 4 lines of Candidate structure, 2 321 lines of content in
all, the remainder headings, blank lines and front-matter.

**Estimated (by reading).** The split of each item's lines among the
five classes below is an estimate made by reading each item once and
assigning its sentences, or a rough fraction of them where a sentence
mixes classes, to a class; the per-item estimates were then summed
per group. The sums were checked against the exact group lengths and
agree within a few lines per group. One outside anchor exists:
THR.0470 records Claude's count of 2026-09-28, operating detail about
320 lines and the three definitions 318. This note's class 3 outside
the definitions comes to about 325 and the definitions to 318 exactly,
so the two readings agree where they overlap. Every estimate should
be read with a margin of about ten per cent; the ranges given in the
answer carry that margin.

**Judgement.** Who the recipient of a forge assignment would be, how
long the assignment would be, and the recommendation are judgement,
marked *(Claude)* where they go beyond what the files say.

The five classes, by kind of content and never by length:

1. **stance + reason** — what the principal holds and why; intent
   content under any design.
2. **directive to the realiser** — wording that says how the engine
   shall behave or be built; the content an assignment carries as
   REQ, CON or OOS, and the content the operating layer already
   carries.
3. **operating detail** — file names, step sequences, field lists,
   exact shapes; content owned by a performing file (a skill, a
   template, a script, an agent, a settings file).
4. **story / history** — how an item was reached, dates,
   measurements, what was tried, which thread it closed; content
   that leaves to the history under THR.0470 step 4 regardless.
5. **delegation / open** — what is deliberately left to be worked
   out; DEL or TBC in an assignment, THR in the intent.

Class 2 is split once more in the answer, because the cleaning of
THR.0470 treats its two halves differently: **2a**, a directive whose
performing file exists as a whole (a skill, a script, a template, an
agent file, a contract), which leaves the item under the cleaning's
rule A; and **2b**, a directive whose only home outside the intent is
a section of CLAUDE.md, or no file yet, which stays in the item under
rule A2 and POS.0120.

## The recipient of a forge assignment

For an ordinary project the assignment goes to recipients who act
without the principal in the room (POS.0130, POS.1350). The direction
of the forge project is realised in the operating layer: CLAUDE.md,
`templates/`, `.claude/` and `scripts/`. Those who realise it are the
principal and Claude themselves, in the same sessions in which the
intent is worked; nobody receives the direction who was not present
when it was decided. The intent says this itself in its last section:
the handover artefacts of this project are the core and the README,
and a `20-assignment.md` would duplicate them for an audience that
does not exist.

*(Claude)* Two readings of that sentence are worth separating.

First, the operating layer already plays the role an assignment
plays for the recipients of any other project. CLAUDE.md is addressed
to the realiser, written in directives, self-contained by the rule
that a reviewer or a command reads it and cannot ask the author, and
carries boundaries without reasons; the research of 2026-09-28 finds
exactly that shape at the bottom of every ladder it surveyed
(findings 5 and 7: the handed-over document carries the boundary and
not the reasons, and must stand without its author where a machine or
a contract reads it). The check `engine` reads the core against the
forge intent — every position honoured, nothing withdrawn still
advertised — which is the job the `essence` lens does between any
other two adjacent layers. The transition intent → operating layer is
therefore already a reviewed layer transition of the chain in all but
name.

Second, a reader for a forge assignment is not impossible, only
absent today. He would be someone realising the forge on other
machinery: a colleague building his own system from the forge's
direction (the fork THR.0360 mentions), a user on another harness
after the engine split (THR.0230), a derivation for another job
(THR.0420). For such a reader CLAUDE.md is not an assignment: it is
the realisation on one harness, bound to Claude Code's skills,
agents and hooks. The one honest case for a forge assignment is that
reader, and he does not exist yet.

## What would belong to an assignment

The groups where class 2 dominates are the content an assignment
would carry: **Structure and style of an assignment** whole,
**Document chain** and **Versioning and state** for what a document
is and how it behaves, **Review** for the contract of every reviewer,
**Operating environment** for the rules of git, of the two doors, of
the two-step output and of the single owner, and the Aim, Partner and
Map blocks of the three **definitions**. Written in the Requirement
style, one idea per item, the same content reads like this *(Claude;
examples, not a draft)*:

- **REQ.0010** Every reviewer shall run as an isolated subagent that
  reads the project's documents only and never the working
  conversation. (from POS.0400, POS.0930)
- **REQ.0020** Claude shall write an artefact once per iteration
  round, on the principal's confirmation, and shall report as written
  only what stands in a named file and section. (from POS.0190)
- **REQ.0030** Every versioned document shall keep its history in an
  append-only companion beside it and never in its body. (from
  POS.0310)
- **CON.0010** The scripts in `scripts/` shall be the only door to
  git, reading state included; the harness shall deny Claude the
  `git` command. (from POS.0550, POS.1200)
- **OOS.0010** Sponsorship, funding, adoption and delivery outcomes
  of a forged assignment are outside the forge. (from POS.0780)
- **DEL.0010** The realiser shall write the state file of each
  artefact's elicitation in the seven blocks of its definition and
  try it on real work before any rule leaves CLAUDE.md. (from
  POS.1380)
- **TBC.0010** (owner: principal) Whether large files are split under
  a master index; decided after the cleaning on the measured length
  of what is left. (from THR.0470)

Three things show in the exercise. First, the directives of the
intent are already in Requirement style in all but the word `shall`:
"Claude never…", "every reviewer runs…", "the ledger carries…" are
directives, and the assignment would change their mood, not their
substance. Second, the REQ items would be the content of CLAUDE.md's
sections in a different order: CLAUDE.md is the same 2b content
written for the realiser, without the reasons. Third, the class 2a
content cannot be delegated away by a pointer: an item must not
depend on an external link to be understood (POS.0290), so the
assignment would have to state what the script or the contract does,
in its own words — the content the performing file already carries.

What would **not** belong to an assignment, under any design: the
603 lines of stance and reason (the intent's own kind, POS.0120); the
story (the history's, POS.0310); the operating detail whose file
exists (that file's); and the open matters, which stay THR until
settled.

## Would the intent get shorter

Three numbers, each a range at the margin stated under Method.

**(i) Lines that leave under THR.0470 step 4 anyway.** Class 2a
(about 330), class 3 outside the definitions (about 325) and class 4
less the reasons in story's clothing (about 300): roughly **950
lines, 850 to 1 050**, about 40 per cent of the content. The intent
after step 4 would stand at roughly 1 300 to 1 450 lines. The first
round of the cleaning, which kept whatever it doubted, took about a
tenth off; the second round, from the rules A to F and A2, is what
this estimate describes, and it is an estimate of the rules' reach,
not a measurement of a result.

**(ii) Lines that would leave only because an assignment exists.**
Class 2b outside the definitions, about 385 lines: the directive
wording whose only home today is a section of CLAUDE.md and which
POS.0120 therefore keeps in the item. An assignment would be the
file of its own that POS.0120 names as the condition for the item to
keep only its aim and name the file. Against it the intent would gain
one pointer line per item that loses wording, about 70 items:
**roughly 300 lines, 250 to 350**, about 13 per cent of today's
content and about 22 per cent of what step 4 leaves. The intent
after both would stand at roughly 1 000 to 1 150 lines. The 165
lines of the definitions' Aim, Partner and Map blocks change nothing
here: they stay wherever they are until the state files exist, and
an assignment would hold them as an appendix or point back to the
intent for them.

**(iii) Lines that would be duplicated.** An assignment complete and
precise by prime directive 8 must carry the whole in-scope substance
of the intent: every class 2 line, 2a included, restated as an item,
plus Purpose, Objective, Terms and the DEL and TBC items. *(Claude)*
At the density of the examples above that is **600 to 900 lines**.
Of them, about 330 (2a) would exist three times — as the aim and
pointer in the intent, as the item in the assignment, and in the
performing file; about 385 (2b other) would exist twice — in the
assignment and in the CLAUDE.md section — which is one copy fewer in
the intent and one copy more beside the operating layer, so the count
of copies of 2b does not change, only their place. The stance and
reason, 603 lines, stay in the intent and are not restated: an
assignment carries no reasons.

**Net effect on the project.** Intent minus 250 to 350; assignment
plus 600 to 900; operating layer unchanged, because Claude reads
CLAUDE.md and the skills at work, not the assignment, and none of them
can shrink on the assignment's account. **The project grows by 300 to
600 lines of text**, and gains one more document to hold aligned with
two others at every change. The saving in the intent is real and
modest; the cost outside it is larger.

## Options

Each option with lines saved in the intent, text added elsewhere,
what it does to prime directive 8 and to the "third copy" argument of
THR.0470, the cost of alignment, and one recommendation line.

**A. Keep `terminal: intent`** (status quo: the operating layer is
the realisation, CLAUDE.md the handed-over direction). Intent saved
by this option alone: nothing beyond step 4. Text added: none.
Prime directive 8 is untouched, since the directive applies to an
assignment and none exists; the ledger's `terminal:` declares the
end honestly and `/forge` and the checks say nothing of a missing
assignment (POS.0160). The third-copy argument holds as THR.0470
states it and this note quantifies it: a third copy for about 330
lines, a moved copy for about 385. Alignment cost: intent against
operating layer, two documents, guarded by the check `engine`. The
one loss: the 2b wording stays in the intent as the only place that
carries it with its reason, and the intent stays 300 lines longer
than it would with an assignment. *Recommendation: keep it — the
only text an assignment alone would save is bought with twice as much
text elsewhere and a third document to align, for a reader who does
not exist.*

**B. A full assignment for the forge** (REQ, CON, OOS, ASM, DEL, TBC
distilled from the intent; the intent keeps stance and reason; the
operating layer derives from the assignment). Intent saved: 250 to
350. Text added: 600 to 900 in the assignment. Prime directive 8 is
satisfied by construction and costs the duplication it always costs;
the third copy becomes a fact for about 330 lines. Alignment cost:
three documents, with the `essence` lens for intent → assignment and
the check `engine` for assignment → operating layer, every change of
substance written in two places and realised in a third, at the
forge's pace of several versions a day. What it buys that nothing
else does: a document a stranger on other machinery could realise
the forge from. *Recommendation: not now — the reader it is for does
not exist, and the day he does (the split, a derivation, a fork), an
assignment can be distilled from the intent then, as POS.1350
describes, with nothing lost in the meantime.*

**C. A thin assignment** (only the DEL and TBC delegations and the
CON boundaries; the rest stays as it is). Intent saved: almost
nothing, since the 2b wording is REQ content and would stay. Text
added: 50 to 100 lines. Prime directive 8 is breached unless the
scope is declared to exclude the realisation — one DEL item, "the
operating layer realises the positions of the intent", would make
the omission an explicit delegation and the breach legitimate; the
document would then be an assignment in shape and a list of
boundaries in substance. The third-copy argument is barely touched.
Alignment cost: a third document, small but still three. The
delegations it would hold already live in POS.1380, POS.0800 and the
threads, with their owners. *Recommendation: no — it saves no text,
adds a document and restates what the threads and two positions
already carry with an owner.*

**D. Treat the operating layer as the assignment formally**
(CLAUDE.md declared the terminal artefact). Intent saved: 250 to 350
if POS.0120's rule is read so that a CLAUDE.md section counts as the
performing file of a 2b directive; nothing otherwise. Text added:
none. Prime directive 8 would then bind CLAUDE.md, which carries no
reasons and is self-contained — the directive is satisfied in
substance today. The third copy is avoided. What would have to
change in the conventions *(Claude)*: `terminal:` would name a file
outside the project, which the ledger's header does not foresee;
CLAUDE.md would need the front-matter of a versioned artefact
(version, status, `last_change`) and a history companion, which it
has not and which the house scheme demands of every artefact; it has
no IDs and no Requirement style, so the `project` check and the
`clarity` lens would have to exempt it or it would have to be
rewritten in items; the `essence` lens would have to read the
transition intent → CLAUDE.md, which the check `engine` reads
today under another name; and POS.0120's A2 rule would have to be
reversed, which the principal set on 2026-09-30 for the reason that a
section may be rewritten and the detail lost. The last point is the
real one: the reason the 2b lines stay in the intent is the
instability of a CLAUDE.md section, and declaring CLAUDE.md an
artefact does not make its sections stable. *Recommendation: no —
it changes five conventions to rename what the check `engine`
already does, and the saving it promises rests on reversing a rule
the principal set for a reason that still holds.*

## Relevance to this project and recommendation

The question has a counted answer. Of the intent's 2 321 content
lines, about 880 are directives to the realiser — the kind of content
an assignment carries — and they sit mostly in the groups Document
chain, Operating environment, Elicitation, Review, Versioning and
state and Structure and style of an assignment. About 950 lines leave
under THR.0470 step 4 whatever is decided about an assignment. About
300 lines, 13 per cent of today's intent, would leave only because an
assignment existed: the directive wording whose only home outside the
intent is a section of CLAUDE.md. An assignment that carried the full
in-scope substance, as prime directive 8 demands, would run to 600 to
900 lines, so the project as a whole would grow by 300 to 600 lines
and gain a third document to keep aligned with two others. The
recipient such an assignment is for — someone realising the forge on
other machinery — does not exist today; for the realiser that does
exist, CLAUDE.md already plays the assignment's part and the check
`engine` already reviews that transition.

**Recommendation: keep `terminal: intent`.** The reason, once: the
only saving an assignment alone brings to the intent, about 300
lines, is bought with 600 to 900 lines elsewhere and a third copy of
about 330 lines of directive, for a reader who is not there; the day
he is, POS.1350 distils the assignment from the intent with nothing
lost in the meantime.

Two proposals for `/forge intent`, nothing written here:

1. **THR.0470, the "Answered" line.** The sentence "it would be a
   third copy beside the intent and the operating layer" could carry
   its count: a third copy for roughly a third of the directive
   content, a moved copy for the rest, the intent about 300 lines
   shorter and the project 300 to 600 longer, by this note's
   estimate. A number where the rule is argued, not where it holds
   (POS.0120), so a one-line pointer to this note in the thread may
   be the better form; the principal's choice.
2. **Candidate structure for assignment.** The section says the
   audience does not exist. It could say when it would: a realiser of
   the forge on other machinery — the engine split (THR.0230), a
   derivation (THR.0420), a fork — so that the condition for an
   assignment is named and not only its absence. Four lines either
   way; a stance of the project, so it stays in the intent and not in
   the ledger.

Neither proposal changes a position; both sharpen a reason already
given.
