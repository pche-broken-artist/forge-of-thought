# Forge of Thought
*A workshop where thought is tempered and shaped.*

## What it is
Forge of Thought is a framework for structured, versioned documents:
an idea travels a fixed chain from a verbatim record of how it first
came out, through a working understanding called the intent, to an
assignment handed to the people who will do the work. The AI elicits
what has not yet been said, criticises and challenges through isolated
reviewers that never see the working conversation, and holds
structure, order and the record. The person decides: all content and
every decision stay theirs, and nothing enters a document because the
AI proposed it.

## What it changes in daily work
- Whoever formulates an assignment no longer works alone against a
  blank page: the AI draws out by questions what is still only in
  their head, asks where it is unsure instead of assuming, and keeps
  the structure, so that the person can think about the content.
- Whoever receives an assignment gets a document that is complete,
  precise and self-contained: structured items with stable
  identifiers, what is out of scope stated as plainly as what is
  wanted, and whatever is left to them handed over explicitly, as a
  delegation or an open question with an owner.
- Quality is worked on before the document leaves: one isolated
  reviewer reads the quality of the documents, another challenges the
  substance of the thinking, and every finding is either fixed or
  overruled with a recorded reason. Nothing blocks - the author
  decides, and every decision stays on the record with its reason.
- As the chain grows, the same way of working reaches business
  analysts, UX/UI and test analysts: higher productivity and better
  quality, with the AI as a cognitive extension of the person and a
  human fully responsible for the output.
- Common standards not only of the output but of its quality: for
  example, every assignment analysed against the same shared security
  requirements, so that every project gets a consistent result.
  Shared material through libraries works today; shared reviewers
  come with the user extensions named below.

## Where it works today, and where it goes
In one company, two pieces of work were written with the forge: the
building of an agentic platform, the company's one governed way to
run AI and automation in production, and the transformation of an IT
department into one that works with AI by design. Both are already
handed over to the people who take them further, and on both, teams
work with the forge themselves. Beside that I use it daily for
further work of my own, an AI strategy among it.

In that company the forge is a foundational building block of a large
part of the IT transformation that lies ahead. Today a handful of
people work with it; after the next feature, the user extensions
below, it is to be ten to twenty, and the end game is about fifty
people who will use it daily as their main tool.

## Where it honestly stands
- Done today: the chain ends at the intent and the assignment.
- In development and testing: a BRD layer below the assignment.
- At the plan stage: a modified variant of the forge for test
  analysts and UX/UI.
- User extensions - one's own reviewer, say a challenger, kept and
  developed in one's personal repository - are what has to be
  finished before a wider rollout.
- The main limitation today: one person works one document. Sharing
  across projects through libraries works. There is long work ahead.
